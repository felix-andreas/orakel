# Two walls, and what they say about where we are looking

Not a request to trade. Two questions about **what the firm is allowed to look at**, both of
which have become evidence-backed rather than speculative in the last two days. The full
table is `ops/idea-funnel.md`.

## The picture at thirteen objects

- **Nine died at wall one:** somebody already prices the object well — Pinnacle, Kalshi,
  DataGolf, FanGraphs, a free Substack, or the crowd itself.
- **Three survived wall one** — and they are exactly the three where no incumbent was found.
  One is in trial (ladder-rv), one is parked with no board until ~08-10, one is the war idea
  waiting on you.
- **Two died at a wall behind it, both in the last two days** — not to a counterparty, but to
  **execution**. Object 12's apparent +5pp edge *was* the bid-ask spread. Object 13's edge
  was real, survived the test that killed 12, and then died on **depth**: a $1.5M board with
  an honest mid, real tape, and a **median $7 resting at the ask on precisely the legs where
  the mispricing lives**.

Wall two is worse than wall one because our screens cannot see it. Depth concentrates at the
mode, mispricing lives in the wings, and the two are anti-correlated — the property that
makes a leg mispriced is the property that makes it unquoted. Every liquidity gate we own
measures the *board*, and every board we have measured passes.

## Question 1 — the war ruling is now gating the scan, not just one idea

You have had `2026-07-26-war-markets-scope.md` open for three days. When I filed it, the cost
was one idea sitting in the backlog. That has changed: **today's top-volume non-sports tape
is overwhelmingly Iran/Hormuz/ceasefire**, so the block now removes a large share of the
liquid, recurring, non-sports universe *from the search itself*. The researcher is picking
its object from what is left.

I am not arguing for a yes. A no is a completely fine answer and I will stop spending scan
attention near the boundary. What is expensive is the open state, because the researcher
burns a cycle a day working around a line nobody has drawn. **Either answer unblocks more
than the idea it names.**

## Question 2 — is a strategy we cannot deploy worth a slot?

Objects 12 and 13 are dead **taker-side only**. In both, the single untested version is
**maker-side** — posting resting orders, which Polymarket charges no fee on. Two consecutive
families arriving at the same untested construction is a pattern, and I would rather answer
it now than discover it a third time.

I had this slightly wrong and want to be precise: **§5 forbids executing, not researching.**
No wallets, no order signing, no exchange keys — that is untouched, and I am not asking you
to move it. But a fill-probability model for resting orders is a research object like any
other, and we already hold the book history to build one. So the real question is:

> **Is it worth a research slot to develop a class of strategy the firm can never run
> without you lifting §5?**

Arguments both ways, honestly:

- **For.** Wall two is where our two most promising recent objects died, and maker-side is
  the only door through it. If the answer is eventually yes, the research has to exist first.
- **Against.** It produces no tradeable result under the current constitution, and a slot
  spent there is a slot not spent finding something we can act on. We have exactly one live
  trial and it is currently losing.

**My recommendation: not yet.** Wait for the 08-02 trial review. If ladder-rv is discarded,
the firm has zero live strategies and the question of what is reachable becomes the central
one rather than a side note — and this is the first concrete answer we have to it. If
ladder-rv survives, we have a working taker-side path and this can stay a note.

No reply needed on question 2 before 08-02. Question 1 is worth an answer whenever you have
five minutes, in either direction.
