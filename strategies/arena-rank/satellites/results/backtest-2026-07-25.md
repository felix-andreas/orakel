# arena-rank/satellites — day-1 backtest

**Date:** 2026-07-25 · **Model:** opus-5 (max effort) · **Trial:** slot 2, day 1

Headline: **the idea's stated mechanism is dead; a different, smaller mechanism on the same
boards is alive.** Gate 0 passes decisively (we can read the resolution variable). Gate 2
kills the joint order-statistic simulation exactly as written — the market beats it at every
checkpoint. What survives is a *shrinkage* edge on the crowd's own distribution: the
satellite crowds are systematically **underconfident in their own favourite**.

The idea's flagship live example was also wrong, for a fine-print reason (§2).

---

## 1. Data availability — the crux, and it resolved better than the idea assumed

The idea recorded "Wayback holds 500 captures of `lmarena.ai/leaderboard*` … and **zero
after 2026-01-28**", and concluded Feb–Jul 2026 could not be vintage-reconstructed.
**That is wrong.** The site rebranded mid-family and the captures continued under the new
host:

| host | text-slice captures (HTTP 200) | span |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lmarena.ai/leaderboard/text*` | 1,788 | 2025-05 → 2026-01 |
| `arena.ai/leaderboard/text*` | 8,132 | 2026-01 → 2026-07 |

Of the exact resolving slices: `text/overall-no-style-control` **103** captures
(2025-08 → 2026-07, every month present), `text/overall` **110**, `text/coding` 92,
`text/math` 55, plus a dense default-`text` series of **7,153** captures used to date each
refresh to the hour.

**Verdict: historical snapshots ARE obtainable for the whole family life. A backtest is
possible.** (Two access notes for the wiki: the CDX endpoint must be called over **https** —
`http://web.archive.org/...` is refused by the egress proxy with a misleading
"host not in allowlist"; and `…/web/<ts>id_/<url>` returns raw gzip bytes, so decode
defensively.)

### Vintage pinning

The table is recomputed over the whole vote history at every refresh, so today's table is
not what resolved a past market. But every rendered page **stamps its own data date**, and
refreshes are discrete (~weekly). So a capture does not have to sit at the check instant —
any capture carrying the data date that was live at the check instant *is* the resolving
table. The dense default series dates each refresh; the sparse resolving-slice series
supplies the table.

- `pinned` — dense series proves which vintage was live and the resolving slice has it: 25
- `bracketed` — captures either side carry the same data date: 22
- `nearest` — best effort, no vintage match: 16

### Parser: three layouts, and a silent-corruption trap

The column set changed three times since 2025-05 (`Rank (UB) | Model | Score | 95% CI | Votes
| Organization | License` → `+ Rank Spread` → `Rank | Rank Spread | Model(+"Org · License")
| Score(±CI, Preliminary) | Votes | Price | Context`). A fixed-index parser reads a *vote
count* where the score belongs on old vintages and fails silently — my first pass scored
Gate 0 at **54%** for exactly this reason. Header-driven parsing took it to 94%. This is the
first-print-vintages failure mode in a new costume: the vintage was right, the *reader* was
wrong.

## 2. Fine print: the idea's flagship example was reading the wrong table

Every 2026 board's rules text names its resolving URL explicitly. For the #1/#2/#3 and
Chinese boards:

> Results from the "Rank" column under the "Text Arena | Overall" Leaderboard tab at
> `https://lmarena.ai/leaderboard/text` **with style control off** will be used to resolve
> this market.

`arena.ai/leaderboard/text` (the default view) has the style-control toggle
**`data-state="checked"`** — style control **ON**. Style control **off** is
`text/overall-no-style-control`, a *different ordering*. Verified in the rendered HTML of
both pages today.

| | default view (SC **on**) | resolving view (SC **off**) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| top Chinese model | `kimi-k3` **rank 10** (1486 ±10, P) | `qwen3.7-max-preview` **rank 11** (1476 ±10, P) |
| next | `qwen3.7-max-preview` rank 19 (1475) | `kimi-k3` **rank 13** (1473 ±10, P) |

The idea read the default table and concluded the Chinese board's
**Alibaba 0.786 vs Moonshot 0.182** was "4.3× the price on the company that is 11 points and
9 ranks *behind* … narrative anchoring". On the table that actually resolves the market,
**Alibaba is ahead of Moonshot**, and the crowd is simply right. The single most-cited
mispricing in the founding document does not exist.

(The #1/#2/#3 answers happen to be Anthropic under both tables, so only the Chinese board
was materially affected — but the reading error was general.)

## 4. Gate results

### Gate 0 — reproduce past resolutions from the archive · **PASS**

| vintage quality | n | reproduced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pinned | 25 | **25 (100%)** |
| bracketed | 22 | **22 (100%)** |
| nearest (no vintage match) | 16 | 12 (75%) |

All 4 misses are `nearest`-quality. Three (2026-03-31) are one case: a refresh landed on the

The fourth (2025-02-28) predates any capture of the resolving slice and is genuinely
unreconstructable. **On instances with a proven vintage: 50/51 = 98%.**

*Venue-epsilon analogue found:* in 1 of 51 instances a refresh landed inside the check
window (dense series showed `Mar 26` at 08:04 UTC, `Mar 31` at 11:35 UTC next day, check at
16:00 UTC on Mar 31) — and the venue used the **fresher** table. Refresh-vs-check timing is
a real adjudication risk, ~2% of instances, and it resolves toward the newer data.

### Gate 3 — is the portfolio effect real? · **FAIL**

The anchor calibration selects `incumbency = 0.0` in 9/10 LOO folds — i.e. the deep board's
implied distribution is best reproduced when a new model's *company* is drawn from the
unconditional historical entrant distribution rather than weighted toward companies already
in the top-20. The "companies are portfolios of correlated models" correction, which is
mechanism (1) of the idea, **adds nothing the anchor wants**. Separately, the naive
"whoever owns rank k today wins" rule loses to the market by more than the full simulation
does, so the portfolio machinery is not what is missing.

## 5. What the leaderboard's uncertainty actually means (the idea's mechanism (2), inverted)

The idea argued: *"the Rank column is an estimate and the leaderboard publishes its own error
bars … casual traders read the integer; the publisher is telling them it is worth ±5 to ±23
places"*, and prescribed putting the published CI into σ in quadrature.

Measured on 11,152 model-pairs across 38 vintages, this is **backwards for the resolving
quantity**:

| horizon | sd(Δ published score), top-25 | mean published 95% CI (implied sd) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–7d | **1.23** | ±5.85 (sd ≈ 3.0) |
| 8–14d | **1.64** | ±5.66 |
| 15–30d | **2.56** | ±5.97 |

The published CI describes uncertainty about *latent skill given votes*. The market resolves
on the *printed number*, which is far more persistent than that. Using the CI as σ
overstates rank uncertainty by ~2.5×, which is precisely why the simulation fades favourites
and loses Gate 2.

**Rank Spread, tested directly** (the idea's "one-hour job"): for top-25 models, median
published spread width is **15 ranks** while median realised |Δrank| one refresh later is
**1**, and only **1.3%** (7/552) finish outside their published spread. Over a full month,
ranks 1–3 move a median of **0 places** (p90 = 1). The published spread is a conservative
bootstrap artifact, not a usable posterior. The crowd reading the integer is closer to right
than a simulator reading the error bar.

Refinements that *are* real:

- **Preliminary / low-vote models are the exception**: sd(Δscore) = **6.98** for Preliminary
  rows vs **1.19** for established ones; **5.50** for <5k votes vs **0.79** for >20k.
- **New entrants dominate**: ~7.7 new top-20 models per 30 days, ~2.5 of them top-5; 20 of
  37 consecutive vintage pairs put a new model in the top-10. This is release timing —
  private information — which `wiki/market-selection.md` says to select *against*. The
  modelable part of this problem is nearly deterministic; the uncertain part is unmodelable.
  That combination is why there is no simulation edge here.
- **Head-to-head persistence** over one refresh, top-30 pairs: score gap 0–2 → leader stays
  ahead 0.79; gap 3–5 → 0.98; gap 6–10 → 0.995; gap 11+ → 0.997.

## 6. What survives: sharpen the crowd's own distribution

Not a model of the world — a transformation of the market's distribution:

```
p_sharp(i) = p_mkt(i)^alpha / Σ_j p_mkt(j)^alpha ,  alpha > 1
```

α fitted **leave-one-month-out** (every scored month out of sample):

| cohort-month | α* | n | market LL | sharpened LL | gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2025-09 | 2.00 | 4 | 0.405 | 0.163 | +0.242 |
| 2025-10 | 2.00 | 6 | 0.387 | 0.163 | +0.224 |
| 2025-11 | 2.00 | 6 | 0.158 | 0.012 | +0.146 |
| 2025-12 | 2.50 | 12 | 0.508 | 0.722 | **−0.215** |
| 2026-01 | 2.00 | 15 | 0.394 | 0.313 | +0.081 |
| 2026-02 | 2.00 | 15 | 0.990 | 0.969 | +0.021 |
| 2026-03 | 1.75 | 15 | 0.325 | 0.132 | +0.193 |
| 2026-04 | 2.00 | 23 | 0.945 | 0.858 | +0.087 |
| 2026-05 | 1.75 | 21 | 0.314 | 0.144 | +0.170 |
| 2026-06 | 2.00 | 21 | 0.201 | 0.043 | +0.158 |

**OOS mean log-loss gain +0.111 (se 0.042, t = +2.63), better in 9/10 months.**
By checkpoint: T−30d +0.162 (t=2.16, 7/9), T−14d +0.083 (t=0.85, 8/10),
**T−7d +0.106 (t=+7.49, 10/10)**.

The edge is strongest exactly where this trial can use it: one week out.

Honest caveats:

1. **It is not the idea's mechanism.** No simulation, no order statistics, no anchor. It is
   the favourite-longshot bias (`wiki/reference/favorite-longshot-bias.md`) measured inside a
   bucket family — and it is **bigger here (6–9c) than the 1–3c that page says usually sits
   inside the favourite's own spread**, on books quoting 0.1–3.7c. That gap is why it is
   tradeable here and wasn't there.
2. **Regime risk.** 2026-02 → 2026-06 all resolved to Anthropic. But the 2025 Google-era
   months (2025-09/10/11) are also positive, and 2025-12 — the one negative month — was a
   Google/xAI split. The bias is not purely the Anthropic regime, but 10 months is 10 months.
3. **Zone mismatch.** The trade buys the favourite. On the July cohort the favourites sit at
   0.93–0.99, i.e. **outside the fundable 3–50c band**. The band-compliant version of this
   trade only exists when a board's favourite is mid-priced (as the Chinese board was in
   2026-04), which is a minority of board-months.
4. **α is aggressive.** LOO picks 1.75–2.5 fitted on log-loss, which rewards sharpening hard
   when favourites nearly always win. Live predictions use **α = 1.75** (low end) with a
   **0.995 cap** — an uncapped α = 2 on a 0.99 favourite implies 0.9999, which no sample of
   10 months can support.

## 8. Verdict

- The **founding thesis is falsified**: the joint order-statistic simulation loses to the
  satellite crowds at every horizon, the portfolio correction adds nothing, the published
  rank uncertainty is 2.5× too wide for the resolving quantity, and the family's dominant
  uncertainty (release timing) is private information the strategy is supposed to avoid.
- The **boards are still worth a trial slot**, on a different and much more modest
  mechanism: a leave-one-month-out-validated shrinkage of the crowd's own distribution that
  survives t+24h delayed execution and a book-quality gate.
- Recommendation to the CEO: **do not promote the variant as specified.** Either retire it
  and graduate the two durable findings to the wiki, or continue the trial explicitly
  rebadged as a favourite-longshot shrinkage strategy — with the honest note that it is not
  the idea that won the slot, its capacity is small, and its in-zone trade is rare.
