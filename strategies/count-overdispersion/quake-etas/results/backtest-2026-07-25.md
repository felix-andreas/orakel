# quake-etas — day-1 backtest and gate run (2026-07-25)

Model: **opus-5 (max effort)**. Code: `src/main.rs` (Rust). Data frozen to R2:
`data/quake-etas-2026-07-25.tar.gz.r2.json`.

Universe: **48 Polymarket earthquake-count ladder boards** (all instances of both weekly
series, 2025-12-22 → 2026-08-02; 45 resolved), 348 legs, plus a **228,900-event USGS M4.5+ catalogue,
1990-01-01 → 2026-07-25** (62,162 events at M≥5.0), plus ComCat version history for 503
threshold-adjacent events.

---

## Verdict — the trial should be killed today

**Gate 3 (the gate the CEO said decides the trial): FAIL, and not in the way we expected.**
The question was "does tradeable edge survive the 0.05 fee in fundable legs (≥3c), or does
it live only in sub-3c wings?" The answer is neither: **there is no edge in any leg band,
because the crowd is not making the error the idea attributes to it.**

The idea's central quantitative claim is that the ladder is "priced as if seismicity were
Poisson". Measured on the de-vigged books of 37 resolved boards at window-open:

| implied Fano (var/mean) of the priced distribution | M6.5+ (n=24) | M5.5+ (n=13) |
|---|---|---|
| **market, de-vigged** | **1.362** | 0.734 |
| 36-year empirical marginal | 1.358 | 0.750 |
| Poisson (thesis says the market sits here) | 1.001 | 0.531 |

The market is sitting **on top of the overdispersed empirical distribution**, not on
Poisson. (The M5.5+ Fano numbers are compressed by lattice censoring — both end buckets are
open — so read that column as a comparison, not a level.) Per leg the agreement is within
about a cent everywhere on the M6.5+ ladder:

| leg | market (de-vig) | empirical | Poisson | realised (24 boards) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.451 | 0.460 | 0.414 | 0.583 |
| 1 | 0.330 | 0.325 | 0.365 | 0.250 |
| 2 | 0.134 | 0.138 | 0.161 | 0.125 |
| 3 | 0.044 | 0.047 | 0.047 | 0.042 |
| 4 | 0.022 | 0.019 | 0.011 | 0.000 |
| 5 | 0.012 | 0.006 | 0.002 | 0.000 |
| >5 | 0.006 | 0.005 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

The upper tail is priced **above** the empirical value (legs 4/5/>5: 0.022/0.012/0.006 market
against 0.019/0.006/0.005 empirical) and the low bucket is within 2% of it (0.451 vs 0.460) —
nowhere near the 1.6× discount the thesis requires at either end. The favourite-longshot
premium (`wiki/reference/favorite-longshot-bias.md`) is visible in the wings and it points
the *opposite* way to the thesis: it makes them expensive, not cheap. There is nothing to buy.

none is seismicity or any natural-hazard category. Smarkets v3 rejects any non-sport/politics

## 2. Gate 0 — resolution reproduction (PASS, better than the idea's number)

Windows parsed from each board's own description (ET → UTC with 2026 DST), recounted from
the USGS catalogue. **Windows are not all 7 days** — `july-14-july-19` is 6 days and the
December/January boards are 8; assuming a week silently corrupts 5 boards.

| family | reproduced | misses |
|---|---|---|
| **M6.5+ weekly** | **30 / 30 (100%)** | 0 |
| M5.5+ weekly | 10 / 15 (67%) | 5, every one off by exactly 1 |

Idea's kill threshold was "M6.5+ below 20/21" — passed on a larger sample (30 boards, back
to 2025-12-22). All five M5.5+ misses are threshold crossings: in four the market's count
was one **higher** than today's catalogue, in one (may-11-17) one **lower**, and each window
contains a 5.5 / 5.59 / 5.68 event or a 5.4 that carried a later `updated` stamp.

**The revision layer, calibrated (not assumed).** ComCat `origin` products with
`includesuperseded=true` give every superseded magnitude with its `updateTime`; the reported
magnitude at resolution is the highest-`preferredWeight` origin available then. Over **503
threshold-adjacent events (2022–2026)**:

| lag after event | n | mean (reported − final) | sd | share revised | share revised ≥0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24h | 500 | −0.0150 | 0.113 | 30.8% | 18.8% |
| **48h** (the resolving vintage) | 502 | **−0.0147** | **0.110** | 29.1% | **17.5%** |
| 168h | 503 | −0.0109 | 0.096 | 25.0% | 14.7% |

Threshold-adjacency census (since 2024): **M5.5 — 1.66 events/week sit exactly at the
threshold, 5.65/week within ±0.10. M6.5 — 0.12/week and 0.46/week.** So the revision layer
is worth roughly ±1 whole bucket per week on M5.5+ and is negligible on M6.5+. That is the
mechanism behind 30/30 vs 10/15, exactly as the idea predicted. The layer is implemented in
the simulator (magnitudes are drawn continuous, perturbed by a bootstrap draw from this
distribution, binned to 0.1, then thresholded) — it is not the reason the strategy fails.

## 3. Gate 3 first — fee, book quality, fundability (FAIL)

**Book gate on the live boards (2026-07-25).** The M5.5+ board is genuinely tradeable: 8
legs, spreads 1.0–4.6c, $2.1–3.5k of resting book each, leg-sum 1.0635. The M6.5+ board is
not, in the places the thesis needs: `0` 0.760/0.790 and `1` 0.200/0.220 are fine, but
**legs 2, 3, 4, 5 and >5 quote 0.1c–3.1c mids** — five of seven legs are below the 3c floor.
The next board (`july-27-august-2`) is two days old and quotes **3–23c spreads, leg-sum
1.237, top-of-book $5–100, with `3` (0.145) above `2` (0.101)** — a monotonicity violation
on a distribution that must decrease. That is a placeholder book, not a market.

## 4. Where the idea's +0.110 log-loss came from (Gate 4 — artifact hunt)

The idea reported **+0.110 log-loss vs the market at "open +6h" on 22 M6.5+ boards (se
0.046, 17/22)**. It reproduces — but only when the checkpoint is anchored to the board's
**creation**, three to five days before the window opens, when the book is a placeholder:

| checkpoint anchor | mean leg-sum (overround) | market LL | Poisson LL vs market | empirical vs market | ETAS vs market |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **board creation + 6h**, M6.5+ (n=30) | **1.429** | 1.448 | **+0.179 (t=2.02)** | +0.202 (t=2.44) | — |
| board creation + 6h, M5.5+ (n=15) | **1.973** | 1.965 | +0.098 (t=0.39) | +0.182 (t=1.10) | — |
| **window open + 6h**, M6.5+ (n=24) | **1.028** | 1.090 | −0.035 (t=−0.58) | −0.023 (t=−0.44) | **−0.070 (t=−1.14)** |
| window open + 6h, M5.5+ (n=13) | 1.146 | 1.762 | −0.161 (t=−0.66) | −0.023 (t=−0.14) | −0.063 (t=−0.22) |

The tell is in the third column: at the creation anchor, **plain Poisson "beats the market"
by +0.179 with t = 2.02** — the very distribution the idea says the crowd is wrongly using.
A test on which your null hypothesis wins by two sigma is measuring the book, not the crowd.
At creation+6h the legs sum to 1.43 (M6.5+) and 1.97 (M5.5+); a de-vigged model scored
against those raw prices collects `ln(sum)` = 0.36 / 0.68 of free log-loss before any
modelling. Once the checkpoint is moved to the actual entry point the strategy specifies —
window open, leg-sum 1.028 — every model's gain collapses and the honest ones go negative.

This is the same failure mode as `phantom-midpoints.md`, one level up: not a dead leg, but a
**dead board** — a listed ladder before the market makers arrive.

## 5. Gate 1 — does ETAS beat a lookup table on the physics alone? (FAIL)

Full ETAS as specified: temporal conditional intensity
`λ(t) = μ + Σ K·10^{α(Mᵢ−M₀)}·(t−tᵢ+c)^{−p}`, M₀ = 5.0, magnitudes from
Gutenberg–Richter (**b = 1.0606**, Aki–Utsu with 0.1 binning), MLE by Nelder–Mead on the
exact likelihood with compensator, parameter posterior integrated over 240 draws from the
curvature at the MLE, magnitude-revision layer bootstrapped from §2, background thinning +
recursive Omori/G-R offspring, **~10⁶ simulated windows per board** (200k sims × 240
posterior draws cycled).

MLE on 1990-08 → 2025-12-01 (62,162 events): **μ = 1.176/day, K = 0.0329, α = 0.615,
c = 0.0096 d (14 min), p = 0.9486**, logL = 42,015. Branching ratio 0.86 over 365 days,
**0.484 over a 7-day window** — subcritical, so the simulation is stable. (p < 1 is expected
when a *temporal* ETAS is fitted to a globally aggregated catalogue: superposing many
regional processes flattens the apparent Omori tail. Noted as a specification limit.)

**Validation — does the fitted process reproduce the real weekly count moments?** 40,000
simulated weeks started from real historical states, 2015–2025:

| | simulated mean | simulated var | simulated Fano | observed mean | observed var | observed Fano |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M6.5+** | 0.958 | 1.321 | **1.38** | 0.889 | 1.244 | **1.40** |
| M5.5+ | 11.02 | 32.69 | 2.97 | 9.414 | 39.62 | 4.21 |

The engine is well calibrated where it matters (M6.5+, the clean instrument) and carries a
+17% rate bias and too little clustering at M5.5+.

**Gate 1, out-of-sample, 602 weekly windows 2015-01 → 2026-07** (ETAS refitted on
**pre-2015 data only**; static models also trained pre-2015):

| | ETAS | empirical marginal | negative binomial | Poisson | **ETAS − empirical** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M5.5+ | 2.8465 | **2.7554** | 2.9321 | 2.9782 | **−0.0911** (se 0.019, t=−4.89, wins 200/602) |
| M6.5+ | 1.2419 | 1.2385 | **1.2330** | 1.2606 | **−0.0034** (se 0.006, t=−0.53, wins 266/602) |

Gate 1's kill threshold was "ETAS must beat the empirical marginal by ≥0.05 log-loss".
**ETAS does not beat it at all — it loses.** The same holds when ETAS is given the unfair
advantage of parameters fitted through 2025 (−0.117 and −0.009).

**Why, physically.** The overdispersion is real but it is almost entirely *within-window
burstiness*, not between-window persistence, and a model that conditions on the past can
only harvest the latter:

| global weekly counts, 1906 non-overlapping weeks | mean | var | Fano | lag-1 corr | R² | residual Fano |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M5.5+ | 9.414 | 39.62 | 4.21 | +0.141 | **0.0198** | 4.13 |
| M6.5+ | 0.889 | 1.244 | 1.40 | +0.074 | **0.0055** | 1.39 |

**Last week's global count explains 0.55% of the variance of next week's M6.5+ count.**
Whatever ETAS knows at window-open about the state of the world, it cannot be worth much,
because the state of the world barely predicts the next seven days at global aggregation. A
mainshock on Wednesday makes Thursday busy — but at Monday 00:00 ET nobody, including us,
knows about Wednesday's mainshock. That is the structural reason this family cannot work at
window-open, and it is the generalisable lesson.

## 6. Gate 2 — ETAS vs the market at window-open (FAIL)

Out-of-sample (parameters fitted to 2025-12-01, all boards later), 10⁶ simulated windows per
board, revision layer on, de-vigged market at window-open + 6h:

| family | n boards | market (de-vig) | **ETAS** | empirical | ETAS − market | empirical − market |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M6.5+** | 24 | **1.0900** | 1.1595 | 1.1127 | **−0.0695** (se 0.061, t=−1.14, wins 9/24) | −0.0227 (t=−0.44) |
| M5.5+ | 13 | **1.7620** | 1.8246 | 1.7853 | **−0.0626** (se 0.282, t=−0.22, wins 7/13) | −0.0233 (t=−0.14) |

Gate 2's kill threshold was "< +0.110 gain, or fewer than 15/22 board wins, or t < 2". We
are at **−0.070, 9/24, t = −1.14**. The full simulation is worse than the lookup table,
which is worse than the crowd.

Per-leg, ETAS wants to buy legs `2` and `3` (0.155 / 0.062 vs market 0.134 / 0.044) and sell
leg `0` (0.424 vs 0.451). Realised over 24 boards: `2` 0.125, `3` 0.042, `0` 0.583 — the
market was closer on all three, and on the favourite it was the model that was wrong.

## 8. What would overturn this

Stated so a future reader can re-open the file honestly:

1. **Gate 2 is n = 24 boards.** A +0.07 loss with se 0.061 is not proof that ETAS is worse;
   it is proof it is not better by the +0.110 the idea needed. What kills the idea is not
   this number alone but its combination with Gate 1 (n = 602 weeks, t = −4.9) and the Fano
   table, which do not depend on the market sample at all.
2. **The finding that would reverse it:** a *regional* ETAS (fit per seismic zone, then
   aggregate) that reproduces the M5.5+ Fano of 4.21 rather than 2.97 and beats the
   empirical marginal on Gate 1 by ≥0.05. Global-temporal ETAS demonstrably under-clusters.
   That is a real modelling gap — but Gate 1 shows the *empirical marginal itself* already
   loses to the market at window-open by 0.023, so a better simulator has to clear the
   crowd, not just clear our own baseline. Given the R² ceiling of 0.0055, it has ~half a
   percent of variance to work with.
3. **Mid-window is where the information actually is** — and it is measured dead: 70% of the
   repricing after a qualifying quake lands within one hour (idea file §2, n=19). We would be
   racing bots on a public USGS feed. Excluded by construction, correctly.

## 9. Reusable findings (wiki candidates)

- **The fresh-board checkpoint artifact.** Anchoring a recurring-market backtest to the
  board's *creation* rather than to the event window measures the placeholder book. Signature:
  leg-sum ≫ 1 (1.43–1.97 here) and *your null model also beating the market*. The
  companion rule to `phantom-midpoints.md`: gate on **leg-sum ≤ ~1.05 at the checkpoint**
  before any log-loss claim.
- **Overdispersion ≠ mispricing.** Fano ≫ 1 in the physics is not evidence the crowd is
  pricing Poisson. Before building the simulator, de-vig the ladder and compute the market's
  own implied Fano — one afternoon, no model. Here it was 1.362 against an empirical 1.358.
- **The persistence-vs-burstiness split.** For any self-exciting count ladder traded at
  window-open, compute lag-1 R² of the count series first. That number is the ceiling on
  everything a state-conditioning simulator can add. 0.0055 here.
