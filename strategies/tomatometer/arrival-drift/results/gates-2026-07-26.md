## Summary

Three independent findings, any one of which ends the variant. They were produced in the
order the charter specified, cheapest first.

1. **Gate 0 (kill).** Kalshi is not "a rival retail venue" on this object — it is the
   **primary** venue by 20–100×, with a ladder 2–5× finer, a **1c median spread** where
   Polymarket quotes 18c, and **its implied score is statistically unbiased for the
   settlement score at every checkpoint from T−96h**. Whatever arrival drift exists is
   already inside the Kalshi line. The idea's own pre-registered kill condition is met.
2. **Gate 3 (independent kill).** The level claim's *direction* is falsified by
   Polymarket's own resolved history. The idea says the crowd's centre sits too high, so
   `P(score ≥ s)` should be over-priced. Measured on 68 resolved ladder boards with per-leg
   ground truth: over-pricing is **zero** on legs below 50c and **negative** — the crowd
   *under*-prices — on legs above 50c, by 10.5 to 29.5 points. What lives in this family is
   the firm's already-validated favourite-longshot bias, pointing the **opposite way** to
   the trade this variant would place.
3. **Gate 5 (independent kill).** The break-even table refuses the drift trade in every
   price band at every checkpoint. The natural form of the trade — buy the cheap NO on a
   leg the score is about to fall through — needs to win **19.2%** and won **3.3%** (1 of
   30) at T−72h.

Two of these were not available to the idea's author: Kalshi's per-leg historical book
(the `candlesticks` endpoint), and per-leg `resolved_yes` ground truth on 68 boards rather
than a resolution bracket on 14 films.

## Gate 0 — the sharp incumbent, and it is a kill

### What Kalshi actually is here

The idea file characterised Kalshi as "another retail crowd reading the same page", and
therefore treated agreement as weak evidence. **That framing is wrong on the facts, and I
am stating it in both directions as the charter requires.**

| | Polymarket | Kalshi |
|---|---|---|
| resolved RT boards harvested | 108 (68 ladder + 40 bucket) | 19 (2026-05-25 → 2026-07-20) |
| rungs per board | 3–9 (median 5) | 10–29 (median 18) |
| board volume, resolved | median **$25k**, max $646k | **$58k – $7.19M** |
| median leg spread at the checkpoint | mixed; 0.8c–18c on the live board | **1.0c** at every checkpoint T−96h→T−6h |
| live Spider-Man `90`-ish leg | 0.650 / 0.830 (**18c**), $219 / $54 depth | 0.69 / 0.72 (**3c**), $137,181 volume, $85,106 OI |

The Odyssey board alone traded **$7.19M** on Kalshi against **$41k headline / $23k taker**
on Polymarket; its `KXRT-ODY-95` leg took **$1.86M**. Polymarket's *entire* resolved RT
history is $11.9M of headline volume across 108 boards.

Strike semantics differ by exactly one point and this must be handled or the comparison is
nonsense: **Polymarket "at least X" = `score ≥ X`; Kalshi "Above X" = `score ≥ X+1`.**
Verified against rules text on both venues and against The Odyssey's settlement (Kalshi
`Above 94` YES / `Above 95` NO; Polymarket `95+` YES / `96+` NO; both imply score = 95).
Both venues settle at 10:00 AM ET on the Monday after wide release, and on 11 of 12
overlapping films the two resolution instants are **identical to the second**.

### The honest counter-reading, stated as instructed

The charter required me to say plainly what this evidence is *not*.

- **Agreement between the venues would have been weak evidence.** It is not what I found.
  I found Kalshi **unbiased against realised settlement**, which is a claim about Kalshi
  versus *the truth*, not versus Polymarket. That is strong evidence and does not depend on
  treating Kalshi as sharp.
- **The Kalshi sample is 19 boards over nine weeks.** The standard errors on the
  implied-median error are 0.12–0.81 points, so this rules out a 2-point bias comfortably
  (it is 2.5–16 se away) but would not rule out a half-point one.
- **A residual cross-venue trade exists and I am not proposing it.** On 11 same-instant
  overlapping films, Polymarket sits **below** Kalshi on matched thresholds: −0.046
  (t = −2.01) at T−96h, −0.033 (t = −1.78) at T−72h, −0.029 (t = −1.70) at T−24h; on
  in-band legs −0.116 (t = −1.99), −0.062, −0.162 (t = −2.65). That is 12 films and 13
  in-band legs — far too thin to trade, and it points the *opposite* way to this variant:
  it says Polymarket's ladder is too **cheap**, not too rich.

## Gate 1 — the checkpoint, corrected

`strategy.toml` fixes the checkpoint no earlier than T−96h. That is right, but the idea's
stated reason is wrong in a way worth recording.

| checkpoint | ladder boards fully quoted (of 68) | boards with ≥1 mono violation | mean implied mass | clean-board yield |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T−14d | 11 (16%) | 27% | 1.0373 | 10% |
| T−7d | 15 (22%) | 27% | 1.0211 | 13% |
| **T−96h** | **48 (71%)** | 4% | 1.0045 | 66% |
| **T−72h** | **66 (97%)** | 6% | 1.0082 | 87% |
| T−48h | 68 (100%) | 9% | 1.0191 | 90% |
| **T−24h** | 68 (100%) | **3%** | **1.0026** | **96%** |
| T−6h | 68 (100%) | 10% | 1.0272 | 88% |

The idea read "6 of 11 boards non-monotone at T−14d" as evidence of an unpriced book. The
sharper statement is that **the boards do not exist yet**: median first quote is 6.6 days
before resolution, and only 11 of 68 ladders have every leg quoted at T−14d. This is the
[checkpoint artifact](../../../../wiki/reference/checkpoint-artifact.md) in its purest
form — not a mispriced board, an *unlisted* one.

Two additions to the wiki page's rule set, both new:

- **Leg-sum on a cumulative ladder is not a vig test.** A ladder is a survival function, so
  its legs need not sum to anything; the honest analogue is the implied bucket mass from
  adjacent-strike differences, which is ≡1 whenever the ladder is monotone. On the 40
  **bucket**-format boards (a genuine mutually-exclusive partition, the family's 2024-12 →
  2025-12 era) the raw leg-sum *is* the arb test, and it runs 1.055 → 1.004 from T−14d to
  T−6h with 30% → 0% of boards above 1.05.
- **T−6h is worse than T−24h, not better.** Monotonicity violations rise 3% → 10% and mean
  implied mass 1.0026 → 1.0272 in the final day, because deep-OTM legs go one-sided and
  their midpoints stop meaning anything. The cleanest checkpoint in this family is T−24h,
  not the last observation before resolution.

## Gate 3 — the level claim is falsified in direction

This is the independent kill, and it is measured against the thing that actually decides
money: per-leg `resolved_yes`, on 68 resolved ladder boards, not a resolution bracket on 14
films. `how-to-make-a-killing` is excluded from every trade-level table (see *Anomalies*).

The idea's level claim is that the crowd substitutes the currently displayed score for the
settlement score, and the displayed score is ~2 points too high. **That predicts a uniformly
positive bias in `price − realised` on every leg**, because every `P(score ≥ s)` inherits an
inflated centre. Measured, in-band and locally-live:

| checkpoint | legs | mean price | realised | bias | se | t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T−96h | 106 | 0.453 | 0.443 | +0.010 | 0.041 | +0.23 |
| T−72h | 110 | 0.471 | 0.518 | −0.047 | 0.038 | −1.22 |
| T−48h | 81 | 0.483 | 0.593 | −0.110 | 0.044 | **−2.47** |
| T−24h | 55 | 0.509 | 0.655 | −0.146 | 0.044 | **−3.28** |
| T−6h | 43 | 0.504 | 0.674 | −0.171 | 0.051 | **−3.34** |

Wrong sign. Split by side of 0.50, the shape of the real effect is unmistakable:

| checkpoint | legs p < 0.50 | bias | t | legs p ≥ 0.50 | bias | t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T−96h | 63 | +0.014 | +0.25 | 43 | +0.004 | +0.06 |
| T−72h | 59 | +0.003 | +0.06 | 51 | **−0.105** | −2.09 |
| T−48h | 44 | −0.074 | −1.05 | 37 | **−0.152** | −3.04 |
| T−24h | 24 | +0.005 | +0.06 | 31 | **−0.262** | −7.77 |
| T−6h | 28 | +0.029 | +0.38 | 23 | **−0.295** | −6.45 |

**The cheap half of the ladder is calibrated. The expensive half is under-priced by 10 to
30 points.** That is
[favourite-longshot bias](../../../../wiki/reference/favorite-longshot-bias.md) — the same
mechanism the firm validated last week in `arena-rank/favourite-shrinkage`, reproduced in a
completely unrelated family. It is not a shifted centre, and it points the opposite way to
this variant's trade: it says **buy** the ladder's favourites, where the drift thesis says
**sell** the ladder.

A note against my own result: this is a calibration test conditional on price, so it is
valid by construction, but the conditioning set shrinks from 110 legs to 43 as the
checkpoint approaches resolution, and the T−24h/T−6h `p ≥ 0.50` cells are 31 and 23 legs.
The T−72h number (−0.105, t = −2.09, n = 51) is the one I would defend, and it is already
enough to reject the positive bias the thesis needs.

## Gate 5 — fundability, and the promotion gate

Per [break-even-win-rate](../../../../wiki/reference/break-even-win-rate.md): `q*` = cost +
taker fee (`shares × 0.05 × p × (1−p)`, `culture_fees`, taker-only, entry and market exit,
free at resolution), `q` = observed win rate, `q⁻` = 95% Wilson lower bound. **Trade only
when `q⁻ > q*`.** Locally-live in-band legs, T−72h, the charter's checkpoint.

**The trade this variant would place** — the score drifts down through the strike, so buy
the cheap NO:

| band (cost of the NO side) | n | cost | `q*` | `q` | `q⁻` | losses/100 to ruin | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0.10–0.30 | 30 | 0.1849 | 0.1923 | **0.0333** | 0.0059 | **−15.90** | refuse |
| 0.30–0.50 | 13 | 0.4460 | 0.4582 | 0.4615 | 0.2321 | +0.33 | refuse |
| 0.50–0.70 | 29 | 0.5586 | 0.5708 | 0.5517 | 0.3755 | −1.90 | refuse |
| 0.70–0.90 | 32 | 0.7968 | 0.8047 | 0.7812 | 0.6125 | −2.34 | refuse |

**Every band refused, and three of four have `q` below `q*` before any interval is
computed.** The top row is the trade in its purest form: buy NO at ~18c on a leg the market
prices at ~0.82, because the score is about to fall through the strike. It needs **19.2%**
and it won **3.3% — 1 time in 30**.

For contrast, the same table on the mechanism that *is* there (buy YES on the favourite
side of an in-band leg):

| checkpoint | band | n | `q*` | `q` | `q⁻` | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| T−72h | 0.50–0.70 | 21 | 0.5458 | 0.5714 | 0.3655 | refuse |
| T−72h | **0.70–0.90** | 30 | 0.8225 | **0.9667** (29/30) | **0.8333** | **CLEARS** |
| T−24h | **0.50–0.70** | 15 | 0.5983 | **0.9333** | **0.7018** | **CLEARS** |
| T−24h | 0.70–0.90 | 15 | 0.8192 | 1.000 (15/15) | 0.7961 | refuse |

The last row is the wiki page's own lesson repeating itself: **15 wins from 15 trades is
not enough to bound an 18% tail.** One band clears at each checkpoint, on 30 and 15
observations. That is a lead for the firm, not a strategy — and it belongs to
`favourite-shrinkage`, not to `arrival-drift`.

## Gate 4 — the simulator, built and kept

`src/main.rs` (crate `arrivaldrift`) implements the arrival-process pricer the idea asked
for, and it is kept even though the gates ended the day, because the object it computes is
reusable and because the shape claim deserved a fair test rather than an assertion.

**It does not Monte-Carlo the terminal score.** Given the checkpoint state `(L, N)`, the
terminal score `round(100·(L+K)/(N+M))` depends on two latent scalars — the arrival
multiplier and the late-pool fresh rate — and on `K | M, p ~ Binomial`. So the exact
terminal distribution over the integer score lattice is a 2-D standard-normal quadrature
(25 × 25 nodes) times an exact binomial pmf in log-gamma space, restricted to ±8σ around
its mean: a few million flops, no sampling error. That matters here specifically, because
**the thesis is a lattice claim** — near a strike the answer turns on one or two reviews —
and Monte-Carlo noise at 10⁻³ is the same order as the effect being priced. `simcheck`
runs a Box-Muller/Bernoulli sampler over the identical generative model and reports the
total-variation distance to the quadrature, so the fast path is verified rather than
trusted.

Three modes are exposed so the claims are separable, as `strategy.toml` requires:

- `frozen` — point mass at the currently displayed score. This is what the idea says the
  crowd does. It is the null the thesis must beat.
- `null` — full arrival process, **zero selection offset**: late critics are fresh at the
  observed early rate. This isolates the **shape** claim (binomial width on a known count
  of not-yet-arrived reviews) with no level claim at all.
- `full` — fitted selection offset `logit(p_late) = α + β·logit(p̂) + γ·ln N`. This adds the
  **level** claim on top.

`fit` estimates both regressions from reconstructed score paths and prints the drift table
by horizon and by checkpoint denominator; `backtest` scores `full` / `null` / `frozen`
against the venue's own ladder on resolved boards with paired log-loss and a t-statistic.

**Verified end-to-end.** On The Odyssey's real T−72h state (`liked = 315`, `n = 330`) the
quadrature and a 10⁶-draw Monte-Carlo sampler over the identical generative model agree to a
**total-variation distance of 0.0057** — i.e. to the sampler's own noise — with means
91.2930 vs 91.2921 and sds 3.9474 vs 3.9587.

The same run is a useful illustration of how much the level claim actually moves, using
plausible (illustrative, *not* fitted) parameters. On the `95+` leg: `frozen` 1.000, `null`
(shape only, no drift) **0.659**, `full` (shape + drift) **0.203**. The drift adjustment is
a **46-point** swing on one leg. The Odyssey settled at 95, so that leg resolved **YES**, the
market had it at 0.875, and the drift-adjusted model would have sold it at 0.20. One board
proves nothing — but it shows the model is not making a marginal correction, and gate 3 says
the correction has the wrong sign.

**The level/shape split was never run to a conclusion, and I am not claiming it was.** Gate
0 and gate 3 had already killed the variant before the historical score paths were
complete, and I chose not to spend the slot's remaining time fitting a model whose level
claim is falsified in direction and whose shape claim would have to beat a venue that is
already unbiased at 1c spreads. The machinery is committed, builds clean, and is documented
so that a future variant with a live thesis can use it.

## Anomalies worth carrying forward

- **A logically impossible Polymarket resolution.** On
  `how-to-make-a-killing-rotten-tomatoes-score` (2026-02-23, $646k board) the `≥56` leg
  resolved **NO** while `≥57` resolved **YES**. The `≥56` leg took **189,907 USDC** of
  notional. The forensics point to a mid-life re-label: 56/58/59/60 each carry ~2,100–2,550
  hourly points and settled at 0.0005, while the `≥57` leg has 22 points and traded
  0.11 → 0.795. The idea file's claim of "zero coherence violations across all 67 boards"
  does not survive contact with the full board set. **This is a live counterparty risk in
  this family**, not a data-cleaning nuisance, and it is excluded from every table above.
- **Venue resolution instants can diverge by two weeks.** `The Invite` settled on
  Polymarket at 2026-06-29T14:00Z with `95+` NO, and on Kalshi at 2026-07-13T14:00Z with
  `Above 95` YES (score 96). Same film, same page, same clock time, different date — and
  the score *rose* through 95 in between. Any cross-venue work in this family must match on
  the instant, not on the film.
- **Fees were off for most of this family's history.** 369 of 556 modern legs carry
  `feesEnabled = false` and `feeSchedule = null`. `culture_fees` at rate 0.05 switched on
  only in 2026-02 → 2026-04. A backtest that applies today's rate across 2024-12 → 2026-03
  over-charges; one that assumes zero under-charges everything after.
- **The family has two structural eras.** 70 cumulative **ladder** boards (2025-10 onward)
  and 40 mutually-exclusive **bucket** boards (2024-12 → 2025-12), with two incompatible
  band conventions inside the bucket era (`80-84` disjoint-inclusive vs `between 50 and 55`
  half-open with ties resolving *up*). The idea's "67 resolved boards" is the ladder era
  only; the true resolved population is 108.
- **`endDate` is not the resolution instant** — it is variously 00:00Z / 10:00Z / 12:00Z /
  14:00Z on settlement day. The real instant (10:00 AM ET) appears only in the leg
  `description`. Anchoring checkpoints on `endDate` shifts every one of them by up to 15
  hours.
- **`liquidityNum` is null on 485 of 556 modern legs.** Do not build a liquidity screen on
  Gamma metadata in this family; read the CLOB book.

## What would revive this family

Not this variant. But the harvest is done and two things in it are worth someone's time:

1. **The favourite-longshot result** (gate 3) is a fresh, independent replication of
   `arena-rank/favourite-shrinkage` in a family with no shared mechanism, no shared crowd,
   and no shared resolution source. It is the strongest cross-family evidence the firm has
   for that bias. It should be tested as an extension of *that* variant, on the same
   `q⁻ > q*` gate, not re-derived here.
2. **Kalshi's `candlesticks` endpoint is a firm-level asset.**
   `GET /trade-api/v2/series/{series}/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks?start_ts=&end_ts=&period_interval=60`
   returns hourly **bid and ask** plus volume and open interest, unauthenticated, for the
   whole life of every market. That is strictly better than Polymarket's midpoint-only
   `prices-history` and it is exactly the historical book that
   [midpoint-is-not-a-fill](../../../../wiki/reference/midpoint-is-not-a-fill.md) says we
   have been missing. Any idea whose object Kalshi also lists now has both a sharp-line
   screen and a historical spread series for free. (`start_ts`/`end_ts` are seconds; an
   out-of-range window returns `{"candlesticks":[]}` with a 200, which silently reads as
   "no data" — that cost me twenty minutes.)
